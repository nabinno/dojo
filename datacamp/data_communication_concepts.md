# 1 Storytelling with Data
## The story begins
```
One of the statements you wrote is False. Can you select which one it is?

[ ]It will be easier for the audience to remember an anecdote on why customers churn than the correlation coefficients between customer traits.
[ ]Your findings will be better aligned with change-averse stakeholder expectations. They will be most likely to implement the program to retain customers.
[x]Even if your data do not reveal a distinct customer behavior, storytelling might influence stakeholders to create the retention program.
[ ]The marketing team will have a better understanding of the impact of your model. It is central since they are creating the retention program.
```

## Diagnose the data story
```
The draft reads:
"Customers on month-to-month contracts churn at a rate of about 95%, far higher than customers on longer contracts. We should focus our retention budget on this group."

Use the app to review the actual churn data and the six storytelling principles. Then decide which principle the draft most clearly violates.

[x]Accuracy: the 95% churn figure does not match the actual rate in the customer data
[ ]Audience-fit: the technical wording is too dense for the marketing team to follow
[ ]Visuals: the chart used to illustrate this finding is misleading and unclear
```

## Translate for the marketing director
```
Translate for the marketing director
Communicatb is taking the churn project to stakeholders. The app shows how a clustering finding was rewritten for the marketing team, using four translation strategies.

A colleague drafts this summary of the churn model for Friday's meeting with the marketing director:

"Our XGBoost model achieved a precision of 0.79 and recall of 0.84 on the holdout set after grid-search hyperparameter tuning."

Which strategy would help the marketing director most with this draft?

Answer the question
[ ]Define the term: explain precision, recall, and holdout set the first time they appear
[ ]Plain English: rewrite the technical metrics in everyday language any reader can follow
[x]Focus on impact: tell the marketing director what action the predictions enable
[ ]Add an analogy: compare the model to something familiar like spam filtering or credit scoring
```

## Be aware
```
Be aware
The meeting was a success! The program director asks you to send your results to the business specialists. You need to write a report and send it by email by the end of the week. You have never met them, so you ask for their background and goals.

After you gather data, you realize you will be communicating your results to a different audience. You want them to understand your results.

Can you select which of the following examples are best practices to translate your results?

Best practice:
- Explain business benefits: Explain that the predictions you made will help target specifically some customers. It will save money for the company in marketing campaigns.

- Include a glossary: At the end of the report, include a page with all the terminology definitions and the acronyms you could not avoid.

Bad practice:
- Anticipate questions: When sending the report, anticipate any questions the business team will have. Answer them thoroughly so they know you are willing to help.
- Explain variable selection: Adjust your content to include some explanation on why you chose your variables for analysis, from a business perspective.
- Avoid analogies: Do not include analogies to explain a concept with simple terms as it could be confusing and disengaging.
```

## Is it a true story?
```
You have done an amazing job explaining your exploratory data analysis on the churn project. Now, it's time to run the model to predict customer churn. You know that you will have to craft an effective story to present these results.

You want to be prepared. So you read your notes on how to build a compelling narrative. But you realized that one of your notes is not accurate.

Which of the following statements is false?

[ ]A compelling narrative is key to presenting relevant insights to your target audience in a meaningful and impactful way.
[ ]Because you should shape the narrative to your target audience, showing only key points or findings is a good practice.
[x]Unless you have a great data groundwork to support your central insight, your findings will need a well-formed and compelling narrative to drive action and change.
```
